If you need an emergency plumber in Morris Township, NJ, call the 24/7 line and a licensed plumber is sent from our Randolph base. Morris Township abuts the southeast border of Randolph, according to Randolph's own master plan, so this is a neighbor and not a stretch.

One point of confusion first. Mail for ZIP 07960 reads Morristown, and ZIP 07961 reads Convent Station, which also accepts Morristown. Morris Township is a separate municipality from the Town of Morristown, so give the dispatcher your street and say township.

Drains

When a drain or sewer line backs up in Morris Township

Many homes here connect to public sewer. The Morris Township Water Pollution Control Utility lists two plants, Butterworth and Woodland, service to about 7,300 residential units, roughly 102 miles of sewer line and eight pump stations. If several fixtures back up at once, the blockage is usually in the house line, and sewer line cleaning is the usual first step.

A single slow sink is a smaller job. We snake the branch, or cable it, and add a camera view when the clog returns. For deep or repeated stoppages, ask about hydro jetting, which scours the pipe wall.

Overview

Dispatch for Morris Township runs around the clock

Our dispatch line is staffed overnight, on weekends and on holidays. Give your street, the nearest cross street and whether water is still running. A licensed plumber is then sent, and the quote comes before work begins.

Hot water

When the water heater fails or the boiler stops

A tank that leaks from the bottom is done, while a tank that only runs cold may need a part. Our water heater repair visits sort out which it is. If the unit is fuel-fired, the township construction office lists a Chimney Verification form, F370, for replacing fuel-fired equipment, so ask the office whether yours applies.

Boilers follow a similar pattern, with pressure, leaks and noise as the early signs. We say plainly when a boiler is beyond a repair.

Morris Township, Convent Station and the districts

The township includes Convent Station and the historic districts of Washington Valley, Pruddentown and Normandy Park, all named on its own website. ZIP 07960 and 07961 both fall inside it. Tell the dispatcher your street and which district or station area you are near.

Leaks

When water shows up and nobody knows where it comes from

A wet ceiling, a warm spot on the floor or a meter that keeps moving with everything off all point to a hidden leak. We use acoustic listening and moisture checks to find the source before cutting anything open, and that is what leak detection means in practice.

The township home page links to the Southeast Morris County Municipal Utilities Authority for water. If pressure drops on the whole street, check your bill and call your supplier.

Before the plumber reaches your Morris Township home

Start with whatever is safe.

1Locate the main valve

Close it if the leak is active. If it will not turn, leave it and say so on the call.

2Stop using fixtures

With a backup, stop flushing and running water so the line does not push more into the house.

3Keep people out of sewage

Keep kids and pets clear of backed-up water until it is cleaned.

4Gas odor

Leave the home, get well away, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Housing age

A township where most homes predate 1980

The township's 2018 housing plan, using ACS data for 2012 to 2016, counts 8,730 housing units. It lists 14.7 percent built before 1940 and 68.7 percent built before 1980. That says nothing about your pipes, but it does make older supply and drain lines a fair thing to inspect, and repiping may enter the conversation if we find worn lines.

Permits

Plumbing permits come from the township office

The Building and Construction Code Enforcement office issues plumbing permits using the F130 plumbing subcode form. Its page does not specifically mention water heater permits, so ask the office before a replacement. Nearby Roxbury and Dover run separate offices.
