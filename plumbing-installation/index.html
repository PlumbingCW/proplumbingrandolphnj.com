Plumbing installation covers adding or replacing the pipes, fixtures and connections that bring water and gas into a building and carry waste out. In Randolph, NJ that ranges from a new bathroom or kitchen to a gas line for a range, a backflow assembly for a commercial property, or the full rough-in for a new house.

New Jersey treats plumbing as licensed work, and the township has its own permit steps. We handle the installation and explain what the permit office needs, so the job passes inspection the first time.

Safety first

Gas lines and gas leaks: leave first, then call

If you smell gas, leave the building at once. Move well away, because gas utility guidance is at least 350 feet, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches, light flames or start a car near the odor. Call a plumber only after the area is safe.

That order matters because a spark from a light switch or phone inside can ignite gas. We never promise a gas response time, since the utility controls whether the supply is safe.

For planned gas work, such as a new line to a range, dryer or fireplace, we size the pipe to the appliance load, pressure test the finished line and leave the connection ready for inspection. Gas is not available at every address, so we start by confirming what fuel your home uses.

Rules

Licensing and permits for plumbing work in Randolph

New Jersey rules say plumbing work shall only be performed by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or employees working under a master plumber's supervision. A single-family homeowner may do plumbing in his or her own dwelling, and unlicensed people may clear drain stoppages and a few other listed jobs. Anything beyond that, hire a licensed plumber.

Randolph's Office of Construction Codes says plumbing modifications or improvements, including replacing bathroom fixtures directly or changing piping, need a construction permit and zoning approval. Water heater and boiler replacement need a construction permit without zoning approval. The plumbing subcode form is signed and sealed by a licensed plumber, unless a homeowner does the work in an owner-occupied single-family home. The office suggests calling while you plan.

Standard

The code your installation is built to

New Jersey's plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, New Jersey edition, adopted September 19, 2022. It sets how pipes are sized, vented and supported. One New Jersey amendment says hot water is potable water not less than 120 degrees F and not more than 140 degrees F, which affects how we set a new water heater.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission urges lowering water heaters to 120 degrees F. Most adults suffer third-degree burns from 2 seconds at 150 degrees F, 6 seconds at 140 degrees F and 30 seconds at 130 degrees F. For households with children or older adults, a lower setting is the safer choice.

Planned testing

Backflow prevention and testing

A backflow preventer keeps water that has left your system, such as from an irrigation line or a boiler, from being pulled back into the clean supply. Assemblies on irrigation systems, fire lines and commercial properties are commonly tested on a schedule.

We install and test these devices as a planned visit. The test checks that check valves seal and relief valves open at the right pressure. If you run a business, our commercial plumbing team handles backflow work along with other building systems.

Ground up

Plumbing for new construction and additions

New construction plumbing happens in two stages. Rough-in goes in before walls close and covers drain, waste, vent and supply lines. Finish comes after, with fixtures, trim and final connections.

Both stages are inspected. Having the layout planned with the builder or architect early avoids moving walls to fit a drain. For an addition, we also check that the existing lines can supply the new fixtures at good pressure.

Local fit

Installing in Randolph's mix of homes and sewer or septic

Township data shows 9,356 homes, with about 54 percent built before 1980. Older houses often need existing pipes inspected before new fixtures tie in, since an upgrade on tired pipe can shift pressure to the weak points. If an inspection turns up worn lines, we will discuss repiping before adding to them.

Where the home sits also matters for drains. The township sewer system covers much of the north and east, including the Route 10 area, while remaining areas are generally on septic. Tell us which applies, because it affects how a new bathroom's waste line is planned. We serve Rockaway Township and nearby towns with the same installation process.

Safety

Carbon monoxide alarms and fuel-burning appliances

New Jersey law requires carbon monoxide alarms in existing covered structures unless there is no carbon monoxide hazard. When we install or replace a fuel-burning water heater or other appliance, we check that venting is correct and that an alarm is present. A working alarm is cheap protection against a failed vent.
