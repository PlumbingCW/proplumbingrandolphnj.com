Your water service line is the pipe that carries water from the curb shut-off into your house. When it leaks, cracks or corrodes through, you may see a wet lawn, a meter that runs with everything off, or a sudden drop in pressure.

Water line repair in Randolph, NJ means locating the failure, then repairing or replacing the damaged length. Because the owner is responsible for this pipe, the repair decision is yours, and we explain the options plainly before work starts.

Curb to house

Water service line leaks and replacement

Randolph homeowners own the water service pipe from the curb shut-off to the house. That means a leak on that stretch is not something the township fixes for you. The Division of Water and Sewer can confirm your meter reading, and then the repair is a plumbing job.

Signs of a failing line include a soft or sunken area in the yard, water at the meter box, discolored water and pressure that drops without a reason inside the house. A buried leak does not always surface, so a meter that moves when the house is idle is often the first clue. Our leak detection methods pinpoint the break so only that spot is dug up.

Before the plumber arrives

A failed service line can drain water out of the house or flood a yard. These steps keep it manageable.

1Close the house main

Shut the valve inside where the line enters. If water still flows at the meter box outside, tell the dispatcher.

2Keep people off soft ground

A washed-out area around the pipe can collapse. Keep children and pets back from the wet spot.

3Photograph the meter

A picture of the dial helps show whether the leak is before or after the house valve.

4Store some water

Fill a few containers for drinking and flushing before the supply is cut for the repair.

Pressure

Low water pressure: where to look

Weak pressure has a few common causes. We sort them by whether it affects the whole house or just one fixture, because the answer changes the fix.

  • One faucet or showerhead only: a clogged aerator, cartridge or stop valve at that fixture.
  • Hot water only: sediment in the water heater or a partly closed valve on the hot side.
  • Whole house, gradually worse: corrosion narrowing the pipe or a pressure regulator wearing out.
  • Whole house, suddenly: a leak on the service line, a partly closed main valve or a problem with the supply itself.

We check pressure with a gauge at an outdoor hose bib, then work toward the fixture that is acting up. If the gauge reads normal at the hose bib, the problem is inside the house and a pipe change like repiping may be the fix.

Public water or well

Is your home on township water or a private well?

Not every Randolph home gets the same water. The township's distribution system also serves parts of Mendham and Chester, a northern section is served by the Town of Dover, and the rest of the township is on private wells. Which one applies to you decides who you call first.

Yard leaks

A wet spot near the meter box

Water pooling around the meter box often means a fitting has failed on your side of the meter. Left alone, the leak washes soil away and grows. We check the connections at the box, test the line and repair what is ours to repair.

If the leak is clearly ahead of the meter on the utility side, we will tell you so and help you describe it to the township.

What the reports say

Randolph's water source and what it means for your line

Randolph buys wholesale water from the Morris County Municipal Utilities Authority, which pumps groundwater from eight wells, six in Randolph and Chester and two in Flanders Valley. The township owns no source or treatment plant. So if something is wrong between the curb and your tap, the cause sits in the pipe, not at a plant.

Township testing found a lead 90th percentile of 0.0033 mg/L across 30 samples in 2023, against an action level of 0.015 mg/L.

Copper came in at 0.127 mg/L against an action level of 1.3 mg/L. Both are below the action levels. Those figures describe the system, though, and an older service line or fixture inside a home is a separate question. If we replace your line, we tell you what it was made of.

Water quality

Softeners, filters and treating your water

Before buying a softener or filter, find out what you are treating. Randolph's published water tables do not report hardness, pH or alkalinity, so we will not guess a number for your tap. You can ask the water division, or test the water at your own faucet.

A plumber installs the equipment and its connections, while a certified lab tests water from a private well. If your concern is rust-colored water or low flow, the pipe may be the cause and a filter would only hide it.

Urgent

When a water line leak becomes an emergency

Call right now if water is surfacing in the yard, the basement is taking on water, or the house has no water at all. Check whether neighbors are affected too. An April 2024 main break in Randolph led to a boil water advisory, and a street problem can look like a failed service line.

Also rule out a toilet, since the township notes a running one can waste up to 200 gallons a day or more and look like a leak.

If a leak on this line has already damaged pipe inside, our plumbing installation work handles the new connections, and we serve Roxbury and nearby towns on the same dispatch.
