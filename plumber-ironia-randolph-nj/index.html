Ironia is a historic village in the southwest part of Randolph Township, along Dover-Chester Road not far from the Chester border. It began as an iron industry community in the 1870s and has its own post office. Today it is a place of single-family streets and a small neighborhood business district.

If a drain is slow, a faucet will not stop dripping or the water heater has failed, this page tells you how we work. Calls go to a dispatcher by phone, and a licensed plumber is arranged for your address.

History

A village built around iron

A regional history of Ironia says it was made a village in 1871 as an iron community, and the iron companies collapsed in an economic downturn in 1873. The township landmark list records homes built by the Ironia Land and Development Company around 1872 at Budd Street, Chester Avenue and Pleasant Hill Road. The Golden Corners House, built in 1826, stands at the corner of Pleasant Hill Road and Dover-Chester Road.

Historic buildings are a fact of the village. They are not proof of what is inside the walls. A plumber should look at the actual pipe before anyone assumes a material or a problem.

Water

Three ways Ironia homes get water

Randolph's housing plan says most properties are served by Randolph's public water. Properties north of Route 10 and east of Dover-Chester Road are served by Dover Water, and the remaining properties are served by private wells. The plan does not say which category Ironia streets fall into, so it is not safe to guess.

Look at your water bill to see who sends it. If you have no bill, you may have a well, and well treatment is a separate subject. A plumber does not test well water, a certified lab does. The state's Private Well Testing Act requires testing when a property is sold.

Zoning

Neighborhood commercial next to single-family streets

The township's land use plan describes Ironia's business zone as a neighborhood commercial district that serves the immediate community, and it describes the surrounding single-family areas as sewered. That older document may have changed, so verify with the township.

Small shops and homes sharing a few blocks means both kinds of calls come in. For a business, see commercial plumbing. For a house, a drain visit is the most common starting point.
