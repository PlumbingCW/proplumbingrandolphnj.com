Repiping in Randolph, NJ

Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes in a house, either the whole system or a long run of it, rather than patching one leak at a time. Homeowners in Randolph, NJ usually start thinking about it after a second or third leak in the same area, rusty or discolored water, or low flow that a fixture swap did not cure.

It is a bigger decision than a repair, so it deserves a straight conversation about what is wrong, what the options are and what you can do in stages. Our team lays that out before you commit to anything.

Is it time?

Signs a repipe makes more sense than another repair

One leak is a repair. A pattern is a reason to look at the whole line. The signs below, especially in combination, tell us a section of pipe is wearing out from the inside.

  • ✓Leaks that keep returning in different spots on the same line
  • ✓Water that runs brown or rust-colored when it has been sitting
  • ✓Pressure that has dropped across the whole house over time
  • ✓Green or white crust, pitting or flaking on exposed pipe
  • ✓Visible patches and clamps from past repairs on the same run

If you are seeing active leaks now, our burst pipe repair team can stop the damage first, and the bigger repiping question can wait until things are dry.

Old steel pipe

Galvanized pipe replacement

Galvanized steel is iron pipe with a zinc coating. Over decades the coating fails, rust forms inside, and the opening narrows until pressure drops and the water picks up color. The pipe can also leak at threaded joints.

We do not assume what any Randolph house is piped with, and a home's age is not proof of its pipe material. If you suspect galvanized lines, ask us to look at the pipe in the basement or utility area and tell you what we see. A magnet and a scratch test on exposed pipe can give a first clue, and we can confirm it.

What to expect

How a repipe is done

  1. 1

    Walk-through

    We trace the supply lines, check shut-offs and pressure, and note where walls or ceilings must open.

  2. 2

    Plan and price

    You get a written scope, the material choice and a clear price before work begins.

  3. 3

    Permit steps

    We tell you what the township needs and who submits what, so nothing stalls the job.

  4. 4

    Replace the lines

    Water is shut off, old pipe is removed and new pipe is run and connected to fixtures.

  5. 5

    Test and restore

    The system is pressure tested before walls close, then you decide on patching and paint.

Most of the work happens in basements, closets and ceilings, though some walls do open. A house is without water for part of the day, so we plan around that.

Permits and rules

Permits for replacing house piping in Randolph

The township's Office of Construction Codes says plumbing changes, including changing piping, need a construction permit and zoning approval. The plumbing subcode form is signed and sealed by a licensed plumber, unless the work is done by a homeowner in an owner-occupied single-family dwelling. The township asks you to call during planning, which is the right time.

New Jersey's plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, New Jersey edition. For a repipe, that means the work is inspected against a set standard, so the permit is not a formality. We explain the sequence, then you or we take the steps the township requires.

Water quality

Lead, service lines and what a repipe does not cover

Randolph's lead results for the water system were below the action level in 2023. A repipe inside your house does not change the water service line from the curb to the house, which the homeowner owns. If you are concerned about lead from an older service line or fixture, ask us to look at it during the walk-through.

Replacing worn interior supply pipe will not change what the utility delivers. It can fix rust-colored water and weak flow caused by the pipe itself.

Older housing

Randolph homes and when to plan ahead

Township data counts 9,356 housing units, with about 54 percent built before 1980 and about 33 percent before 1970. A house in that group has had decades of water pressure, thermal cycling and fixture changes. That makes a planned inspection a smart move before a leak forces the issue, since a repipe done on your schedule is easier than one done during a flood.

If a leak ever involves the buried line, a water line repair visit looks at that section, and a repipe covers the pipes indoors. We work with homeowners across Morris Township and nearby towns on the same basis.

Phased options

Repiping in stages

You do not always need the whole house at once. A kitchen and bath group, one floor or the basement main run can be replaced first if the budget calls for it. We will tell you which section matters most and what staging does to the permit and the total price.

The trade-off is more visits and more openings in the walls. If your house has many problem spots, doing it in one project is usually cleaner.

Estimate factors

What drives a repiping quote

Repiping is quoted from a walk-through, not over the phone, because the house decides the work. You see the scope and the price in writing before anything starts.

More bathrooms, sinks and appliances mean more pipe and more connections.

Copper, PEX and CPVC differ in material cost and in installation time.

Open framing is quick. Finished plaster, tile and tight crawl areas add time.

A multi-level house with long vertical runs takes more planning than a single level.

Drywall repair and paint are separate from the plumbing and can be done by your own contractor.

Insurance generally pays for sudden damage, not for replacing pipes that are simply old. Some policies pay for the leak damage but not for the repipe itself. Review your policy, and ask your agent how it treats a pipe failure.
