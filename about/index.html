About Randolph Pro Plumbing

A call-only plumbing line on Route 10 that sends licensed plumbers to Randolph and nearby Morris County towns.

What we do

Randolph Pro Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing line based at 246 Route 10 in Randolph. You call, a person answers, and a licensed plumber is dispatched to your address. There are no online forms, so everything starts with a conversation.

We handle the same problems most Randolph households eventually meet: clogged drains, failed water heaters, hidden leaks, sewer backups, flooded basements and cold boilers. The services page lists every one.

How a call goes

Every call follows the same short path, whether it is a burst pipe at midnight or a water heater you want swapped this week. Knowing the steps ahead of time helps you give the dispatcher what they need quickly.

  1. 1

    You describe the problem

    Say what you see, where it is and whether water, gas or sewage is involved.

  2. 2

    We confirm the address and area

    The dispatcher checks that your street is inside the service area and notes access details.

  3. 3

    A plumber is sent

    A licensed plumber is assigned, and you are told when to expect them if the schedule allows a firm window.

  4. 4

    You approve the price first

    The plumber explains the fix and the cost before work starts. You decide.

Licensing and how to check it

New Jersey law says plumbing is to be performed by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor, or employees working under a master plumber's supervision. Homeowners may work on their own single-family dwelling.

You do not have to take anyone's word for a license. The Division of Consumer Affairs runs a license verification system at njconsumeraffairs.gov, and checking it takes a minute.

Calls to our line may be recorded for quality and training purposes.

Where we work

Randolph is home base, and the line also dispatches to Dover, Denville, Roxbury, Rockaway Township and Morris Township. See the service areas page for ZIP codes and local notes.

Why the line is call-only

A plumbing problem is hard to describe in a form field. Is that a drip or a spray? Is the water clear or brown? Is the smell sewage or gas? A short conversation settles those questions in a minute, and the answers change who is sent and what they bring.

You do not need account numbers or a booking reference. A street address and a short description of what is wrong are enough to start.

Calling also means nobody waits for an inbox to be checked. If a pipe lets go at 2 a.m., the call connects to a person who can start the dispatch, not a ticket that sits until morning.

Randolph details the plumber should already know

Randolph does not run on one water system. The township buys water from the Morris County Municipal Utilities Authority for many streets, while other homes use private wells. Sewer is public in much of the north and east, including the Route 10 area, and septic systems serve the rest. Which one you have changes what a clog or a smell means.

The township's Office of Construction Codes says water heater replacement and boiler replacement need a construction permit, and that changing piping or replacing fixtures needs a permit plus zoning approval. We tell you that before planned work, not after.

About 54 percent of the township's housing units were built before 1980, per the township's housing plan. That is why you will hear us talk about original supply lines, older water heaters and drain lines that have had decades to collect scale and roots. We cannot see inside your walls from here, so we look before we recommend.

Safety comes before the repair

If you smell gas, do not call us first. Leave the building, move well away, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside. We will happily take the plumbing call after the gas company clears the house.

If water is near electrical outlets, an appliance or the panel, stay out of it. Shut the main water valve if you can reach it with dry feet, and call. If you cannot reach it safely, tell the dispatcher and they will walk you through the next step.

What we do not do

We do not do waterproofing excavation, well drilling, septic pumping or full HVAC replacement. If your problem belongs to another trade, we say so instead of guessing.
