Our team dispatches licensed plumbers for businesses along Route 10 and the roads around it. We start with a plain assessment: what is failing, what it is costing you each hour, and what must happen tonight versus what can wait for a scheduled visit.

Emergency plumbing problems that stop a business

A backed-up restroom during business hours, sewage on a kitchen floor, a burst line in a ceiling or no hot water in a food-service space can force a closure. Health departments and landlords have their own rules, and an operator rarely wants to find out what they are while water is still running.

Our job is to stop the damage first, restore service second, and explain the permanent fix third. Be ready to tell the dispatcher what the building is, who holds the lease and where the main shutoff is.

Leaks in commercial buildings

A restaurant, a medical office and a warehouse leak differently. Pipes run above drop ceilings, inside walls, under slabs and in mechanical rooms, and many are shared with neighboring tenants. Finding the source before cutting anything is cheaper than opening five ceilings.

That is the work of leak detection, using moisture mapping, acoustic listening and pressure tests. If the water meter keeps moving after everything is shut off, the leak is between the meter and the building or inside it, and we narrow it down before any digging.

Grease traps and storm drains are different systems. The trap protects the sewer from fats. Storm drains carry rain, and clogged ones flood lots and loading docks. Both need a plan, not a reaction.

After hours

What to do when a commercial line fails after closing

Staff or a night manager can limit damage before a plumber arrives. Share this list with whoever locks up.

  1. Find the building main valve and close it if water is active. If it will not turn, leave it alone and tell the dispatcher.
  2. Keep people and customers away from backed-up sewage and wet floors. Put out wet-floor signs.
  3. If there is a gas odor, evacuate, move well away, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before calling us.
  4. Photograph the damage and note the time you found it. Your insurer and landlord will ask.
  5. Tell the dispatcher which shutoffs are shared with other tenants, since a building owner often controls those.

Backflow, new fixtures and planned work

Restaurants and other commercial buildings often have backflow devices that protect the drinking water. Testing and installation sit under plumbing installation, which we schedule rather than treat as an emergency.

If your business needs a permit for new plumbing, the Randolph Office of Construction Codes is the place to ask. A business in Dover asks its own town instead. Water and sewer account questions in Randolph go to the Division of Water and Sewer. After hours, the township routes water emergencies through police dispatch.
