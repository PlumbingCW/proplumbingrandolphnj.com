Water heater repair in Randolph, NJ means finding the one failed part, such as a thermostat, heating element, burner assembly, valve or fitting, and fixing it so the tank keeps working. Sometimes the part is cheap to swap and the tank has plenty of life left. Sometimes the tank itself has rusted through and no repair will hold.

We tell you which one it is before any work starts. Call the 24/7 line and a dispatcher sends a licensed plumber to check the heater, the connections and the shutoffs.

Act first

When a leaking water heater becomes an emergency

A steady stream from the tank, water spreading across a finished basement, or a ceiling stain under an upstairs unit all call for a same-day visit. The longer the water runs, the more drywall, flooring and stored boxes it reaches. A few drips from a pipe fitting can wait for a scheduled visit, but a puddle that grows cannot.

Shut the cold supply valve above the heater first. Then stop the gas or switch off the breaker, as covered below, and call us.

Water on the floor around the tank? Call the 24/7 line and describe where it is coming from.

Call for dispatch

Before the plumber arrives

Four quick moves limit the damage and keep you safe.

1Close the cold supply

The shutoff sits on the pipe feeding the top of the tank. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If it spins freely or leaks, tell the dispatcher.

2Cut the fuel or power

Set a gas heater's control to off, or flip the breaker for an electric one. Never leave an electric element energized in a tank that has drained.

3Clear the floor

Move boxes, rugs and electronics out of the splash zone. A wet vac or towels buy time if the leak is slow.

4Smell gas? Leave

Walk outside, move well away from the house and call the gas utility or 911 from there. Do not flip switches on the way out.

Find the source

Leaking from the top, the side or the bottom

Top of the tank

Usually a loose or corroded inlet, outlet or anode fitting. These are often repairable with new connectors or a fresh seal.

Side of the tank

Often condensation or a weeping element gasket on an electric unit. If water seeps from the jacket itself, the inner tank may be failing.

Bottom of the tank

A dripping drain valve can be swapped. Water coming out from under the whole tank usually means the shell has rusted through.

Relief valve pipe

A drip from the discharge pipe means the valve is opening on pressure or heat. It can be a worn valve or a symptom of a bigger problem.

Step by step

No hot water and how we trace it

Cold showers are the most common reason people call us about a water heater. The cause depends on the fuel. A gas unit with a dead pilot or failed thermocouple behaves differently from an electric unit with a tripped high-limit switch.

  1. 1

    Confirm power or fuel

    We check the breaker, the gas supply and the control settings before opening anything.

  2. 2

    Test the parts

    A multimeter shows whether an electric element or thermostat has failed. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and flame sensor.

  3. 3

    Check the tank

    We look for moisture, rust streaks and a damp insulation jacket. These tell us whether new parts are worth installing.

  4. 4

    Explain the choices

    You hear the repair option and the replacement option, with the reason for each, before we pick up a wrench.

What shapes the cost of a water heater repair

A thermostat swap and a relief valve change are different jobs, so there is no single number. After the plumber inspects the unit you get a clear price before work starts.

Elements, thermostats, valves and burner parts differ in cost and in how long they take to install.

Gas units add venting and combustion checks. Electric units add electrical testing.

A heater in a tight closet or crawlspace takes longer to work around than one in an open basement.

Safety

Water temperature, scalds and the thermostat dial

The Consumer Product Safety Commission urges setting water heaters to 120 degrees F. At 150 degrees F, most adults suffer a third-degree burn in about 2 seconds, and at 140 degrees F it takes about 6 seconds. New Jersey's plumbing subcode also sets hot water at no less than 120 and no more than 140 degrees F, so we set repaired units inside that range.

If you have small children or older family members at home, ask us to confirm the setting at the end of the visit. A bad thermostat can run hot as easily as cold.

Paperwork

Repairs, permits and the township

Randolph Township's Office of Construction Codes requires a construction permit for water heater replacement, but routine repair is treated as ordinary maintenance under state rules and is generally exempt. Swapping an element or valve is repair. Pulling out the tank is not. If a visit turns into a replacement, our plumber walks you through the permit step and the township's Office of Construction Codes is the place to ask.
