Why Does My Toilet Keep Clogging?

One clog is bad luck. Three in a month is a pattern, and the pattern points to a cause you can find.

A toilet that keeps clogging is rarely just unlucky. Repeat clogs come from one of four places. The toilet itself has weak flush power, something is going down that should not, the drain line behind it is partly blocked, or the main line or septic system is struggling. In Randolph, NJ, sorting out which one it is saves you from plunging the same bowl every weekend.

This guide starts with what the symptoms mean, then covers what not to flush, how to use a plunger and an auger properly, and the signs that the problem is bigger than the bowl.

Read the symptoms before you grab the plunger

Where the clog shows up tells you how deep it is. If only one toilet is slow and the sink and shower beside it drain fine, the blockage is in that toilet or its short branch. If the tub gurgles when you flush, or a basement floor drain bubbles, the problem sits farther down the line.

Causes of a toilet that clogs again and again

Weak flush

Older and low-flow toilets can lose power when mineral scale blocks the rim jets or the siphon hole. The bowl then empties slowly and lets waste stall in the trap.

Too much paper

Thick or large amounts of tissue can overwhelm even a healthy toilet. It is the most common household cause, and the easiest to change.

Wrong items

Wipes, hygiene products, paper towels and cotton balls do not break down the way toilet paper does. They hang up in the trap and snag everything that follows.

Problem downstream

Roots, a bellied section of pipe or buildup on the walls of the drain can cut flow. The toilet then clogs because the line behind it cannot carry the load.

What should never go down the toilet

Toilet paper and human waste are the only two things a toilet is built to move. Flushable wipes are labeled that way, but they often fail to break apart in the pipe. Treat the label with doubt.

  • Wipes of any kind, including those sold as flushable
  • Feminine products, diapers and cotton swabs
  • Paper towels, tissues and dental floss
  • Cat litter, even the kind sold as flushable
  • Grease, cooking oil and medications

A household with young children or a guest bathroom that sees irregular use benefits from a small covered wastebasket beside the toilet. It costs little and removes the temptation.

Plunger or auger: which tool for which clog

A flange plunger, the kind with a rubber lip that folds out, seals better in a toilet than the flat cup type. Fill the bowl enough to cover the cup, seat it, and push gently first to push out the trapped air. Then pull and push with steady strokes for about a dozen cycles.

A closet auger, which has a short cable and a curved guide, goes where a plunger cannot reach. Feed it into the trap, turn the handle and pull back any plug it catches. Skip chemical drain openers. They rarely fix a toilet clog, they can damage fittings, and splashing caustic liquid is a hazard if the bowl then has to be cleared by hand.

A simple ladder for repeat clogs

  1. 1

    Check the basics

    Confirm the tank fills to the marked line and that the flapper seals. Low water in the bowl means less force on each flush.

  2. 2

    Clear the trap

    Use the flange plunger first and the closet auger second. If the bowl drains with a strong swirl afterward, the fixture is clear.

  3. 3

    Test the neighbors

    Run the sink and shower beside the toilet. Any gurgle or slow drain means the clog sits in the branch line, not the bowl.

  4. 4

    Call before it escalates

    Two clogs in a month, a gurgling tub or a sewage smell all justify a camera look. A drain cleaning visit finds the cause instead of treating the symptom.

When the problem is the sewer line

Tree roots slip into small joints in older clay or cast iron pipe and grow into a mass that catches paper. Homes with a long run to the street or a past history of backups are the usual candidates. You may also hear gurgling after laundry or see water rise in a floor drain.

A camera inspection shows the cause. Cutting heads on a drain machine can clear roots and buildup, and a sewer line cleaning is the right step when more than one fixture is affected. If the pipe is cracked or collapsed, the conversation shifts to lining or replacement, and the camera video helps you decide.
