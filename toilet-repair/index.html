Toilet repair in Randolph, NJ covers four zones: the parts inside the tank, the seal under the bowl, the supply line on the wall and the drain behind it. Water enters through a fill valve, drops through a flapper into the bowl, and leaves through a trap that keeps a water seal against sewer odor.

When one piece fails you get a hiss that never stops, a weak flush, a puddle at the base or water climbing toward the rim. We send licensed plumbers who find which piece failed, fix that piece, and tell you plainly when the whole toilet should go.

Quiet and costly

A running toilet shows up on the water bill first

Randolph's own high-water-bill guidance names a running toilet as the most common cause of a surprise bill, and says one can waste up to 200 gallons a day or more. That is a leak you never see because the water goes straight down the drain. If your bill jumped, check the toilets before anything else.

The township suggests a dye test. Drop color into the tank, wait about 20 minutes, and if color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. A faulty fill valve is the second most common cause on the same page. Both are small parts, so the fix is usually quick once someone confirms which one it is.

When an overflowing toilet is an emergency

A bowl rising toward the rim, water spreading across the floor, or a toilet that backs up when you flush another fixture is urgent. Stop flushing. Do not plunge a bowl that is already full of rising water, and keep children and pets out of the room.

Water that comes up in a tub or floor drain at the same time points past the toilet to the main line. That is a sewer problem, not a fixture problem, and we handle it differently.

Before the plumber gets there

Two minutes of prep keeps the damage small.

1Find the stop valve

Look behind or beside the bowl for the small oval handle on the wall pipe. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

2Lift the tank lid

If the tank is still filling, hold the float up or lift the rod. That stops new water from entering the bowl.

3Soak up the floor

Towels along the base slow water that heads for the ceiling below. Keep the bathroom fan off and power strips away from the wet area.

4Note what you saw

Tell the dispatcher if other drains gurgled, whether the flush was weak first, and how old the toilet is.

The usual suspects

Four parts that cause most toilet repairs

  1. 1

    Flapper

    The rubber seal at the bottom of the tank warps or gets slimy and lets water creep into the bowl. The toilet refills every few minutes with a faint trickle.

  2. 2

    Fill valve

    The tower on the left that refills the tank. When it sticks, the tank overfills, hisses, or never shuts off.

  3. 3

    Wax ring or flange seal

    A ring of wax or rubber between the toilet and the floor flange. When it fails, you see water around the base and sometimes a soft floor or a sewer smell.

  4. 4

    Supply line

    The braided hose from the wall to the tank. A corroded or loose line drips slowly or, rarely, lets go all at once.

Cost factors

What changes the cost of a toilet visit

Cost depends on what failed and what it takes to reach it. You get a clear price before work starts, and a swap of two tank parts looks very different from a reset with a new flange.

A flapper or fill valve is a short job. A broken flange or damaged supply valve takes longer and needs more parts.

A soft or stained subfloor around the base has to be dealt with before a toilet can sit on it again.

Older units can have discontinued parts, which pushes the decision toward replacement.

Overnight and weekend dispatch costs more than a daytime visit, and the dispatcher says so on the phone.

Policies differ. Many treat a sudden overflow differently from slow seepage that went on for weeks. Read your policy, take photos before you mop, and ask your insurer what documentation it wants.

Replacing a toilet and the township permit page

Randolph's construction permit page says that replacing bathroom fixtures and changing piping needs a construction permit and zoning approval. Routine repair is treated differently under state permit rules, and the local enforcing agency decides where the line sits. A new flapper is maintenance, but a toilet swap may not be.

Before you buy a new toilet, call the Randolph Office of Construction Codes while you are still planning. They can tell you what your job needs. A licensed plumber signs the plumbing subcode form unless you are an owner doing the work yourself in your own single-family home. Homeowners in Dover should ask their own town's office, since Randolph's page covers Randolph only.

When the problem is the drain, not the toilet

A toilet that clogs again a day after a plunger, gurgles when the shower runs, or backs up with another fixture is reporting a blockage further down. Our drain cleaning crew clears branch lines with a cable and camera. If the whole house backs up, the cause may sit in the main line, and sewer line cleaning is the right next step.

A worn toilet is often not the only tired fixture in a bathroom, so the same visit can cover faucet repair at the sink or tub.
