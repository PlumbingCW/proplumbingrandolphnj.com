Denville Township counted 17,107 residents in the 2020 Census, and 87.7 percent of homes are owner-occupied. Most callers are owners making their own repair decisions, so we explain the problem plainly, price it before work begins, and leave the choice with you.

The river

The Rockaway River shapes Denville's basement problems

Morris County's hazard plan says flooding along the Rockaway River occurs virtually throughout the entire length of the river within the township. It puts about 1,013 residents (6.0 percent) and 503 buildings (7.0 percent) in the one percent annual chance floodplain, and counts 57 repetitive loss properties plus 19 severe repetitive loss properties. Tropical Storm Irene in August 2011 caused severe flooding across the county.

What that means for you depends on your lot. A low basement should have a sump that starts on its own, a discharge line that runs clear, and ideally a battery backup. Our sump pump repair crews fix the pump and the piping. We do not stop river water, and if the river itself is in your house, call township emergency services first.

Utilities

Township water, sewer authority service and septic: know which you have

Denville Township runs its own Water Department from 140 Morris Avenue. A 2003 state source water assessment lists township-owned wells in glacial sand and gravel aquifers, plus purchased supply, and says the system also reaches a few customers in nearby towns, Randolph among them. For a pressure drop or a discolored tap, call the Water Department too, since it may be a main issue and not your pipe.

Sewer is less uniform. Denville parcels sit in the Rockaway Valley Regional Sewerage Authority service area, yet a 2021 state notice about one lot said sewer service cannot be assumed without an expansion of that area. The township construction page also describes septic plan review through the Health Department. Before we scope a drain or sewer line cleaning job, we ask which system your house uses.

What to do while a crew heads to Denville

Four quick moves that limit damage.

1Shut the water if you can

Find the main shutoff and turn it clockwise. If the basement already holds water, do not wade in near the panel.

2Power down wet equipment

Turn off the sump or water heater only at a dry breaker. Never touch a switch while standing in water.

3Photograph the damage

A few photos of the leak and the wet area help later with insurance questions.

4Gas odor means leave

Walk outside, get well away, and call the gas utility or 911. Plumbing waits until the building is safe.
