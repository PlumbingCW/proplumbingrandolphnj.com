Sewer line cleaning in Randolph, NJ is the work of clearing the main pipe that carries waste from your house out to the public sewer or a septic tank. When it clogs, every fixture in the home is affected, and the first sign is often water in the lowest drain.

Randolph Pro Plumbing sends licensed plumbers at any hour. They bring cable machines, jetting equipment and a drain camera, and they will tell you plainly if your home is on septic, where the answer changes.

Sewer backup

Sewage coming up in the basement or lowest drain

Waste water always finds the lowest opening. In most houses that is a basement floor drain, a laundry sink or a ground-floor tub. If you see brown water, smell sewage, or hear gurgling from several drains, the main line is blocked or partly blocked.

Stop all water use at once. Keep the area clear of children and pets, and do not run a wet vacuum on sewage. Then call, because the water will keep coming as long as anyone flushes or washes.

Do these things first

  1. Stop using toilets, sinks, showers and the washer.
  2. Switch off power to the basement if water is near outlets and you can reach the panel dry. If not, stay out and tell the dispatcher.
  3. Move valuables off the floor.
  4. Open a window if the smell is strong.
  5. Write down which fixtures were backing up and when.

The notes further down cover access to the line.

What blocks a sewer line

Roots

Fine roots enter at joints and thicken into a net that catches paper and grease.

Grease and wipes

Grease coats the wall. Wipes sold as flushable do not break down like paper.

Scale and sediment

Mineral and waste film narrow the bore until a small load jams it.

Pipe damage

A sag, offset or crack holds water and solids, so cleaning gives only temporary relief.

For heavy grease and scale we usually move to hydro jetting after the cable has opened the line.

Which sewer serves your home

Randolph's public sewer is not a single system. The RVRSA service area covers most of the north and east parts of the township, including the Route 10 area. The southern half along Sussex Turnpike drains to Morris Township's Butterworth plant. Remaining un-sewered areas generally use septic systems.

This matters on a backup call. If you are unsure which applies, tell the dispatcher your street. A blockage in the house pipe is ours to clear, while a problem in the public main is a call to the township's Division of Water and Sewer. Butterworth is a Morris Township plant, and our dispatch also covers Morris Township itself.

Septic homes: what we do and what we do not

On a septic property we clear the pipe between the house and the tank, camera the line and tell you if it is sagging, cracked or root-bound. We do not pump or empty tanks. A septic hauler does that, and we will say so if the tank looks like the problem.

Morris County Health notes that DEP recommends inspecting and pumping a typical septic system every 3 to 5 years. If you cannot remember the last pumping, a slow house drain may be the tank speaking, so mention it when you call.

Prep notes for the plumber's arrival

1Find the cleanout

It is a capped pipe in the basement, crawlspace or yard. Uncover it if you can.

2Stay out of the water

Sewage carries bacteria. Wear boots and gloves if you need to walk through it.

3Keep pets away

Machines and standing water are not safe for animals or small children.

4Say if it happened before

A repeat backup suggests damage or roots, which changes the plan.

What affects the bill for cleaning a sewer line

Cleaning a main takes bigger machines than a sink clog, so expect a higher figure than basic drain work. The price is stated before the crew begins.

A plug near the cleanout takes a short run. One near the street needs the full length.

Without one, a toilet may have to come off the floor to reach the line.

Roots and compacted debris take longer than soft waste.

Septic properties may need extra checks of the tank connection.

Night, weekend and holiday dispatch is priced differently from daytime visits.

Check your policy before you assume cover. Some homeowners policies exclude sewer backup unless you bought an endorsement, and a sudden event may be treated differently from slow seepage. Take photos before you clean up.
