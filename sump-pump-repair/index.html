Sump pump repair in Randolph, NJ is about one machine doing one job: lifting water out of a pit in the basement floor and sending it outside before it reaches the slab. A sump pump sits in a basin, rises on a float switch when water comes in, and pushes the water up a discharge pipe past a check valve.

When it fails, nothing warns you. The basement is dry until the storm comes, and then it is not. We send licensed plumbers who test the pump, switch, check valve and discharge, repair what is salvageable, and replace what is not.

Basement taking on water right now

Water rising in the pit or spreading across the floor means the pump has failed, been overwhelmed or lost power. Stay out of the water if outlets, the panel or a furnace are nearby. Electricity and water do not mix, and a flooded basement is a shock hazard.

If the panel is in the wet area, do not go in. Call the line, tell the dispatcher what you can see from the stairs, and we plan the visit around it.

4Tell us the weather

Say whether it is raining, whether the pump ran earlier, and whether it ever shuts off.

Why pumps fail

Four faults that take a sump pump out

Stuck float

The float jams against the side of the basin, so the pump never starts or never stops.

Burned-out motor

A pump that runs dry or runs constantly for years eventually overheats.

Clogged intake

Silt, gravel and debris block the screen, and the motor strains.

Failed check valve

Water in the discharge pipe drops back into the pit and the pump cycles forever.

Backup pumps and check valves

FEMA's basement flooding guide calls a battery backup pump an inexpensive safeguard against flooding. It also warns that check valves can jam open with debris, which defeats their purpose. FEMA suggests asking local officials whether certain valves are permitted.

In practice, that means a second pump on its own float, a battery sized to run through an outage, and a check valve that gets inspected instead of forgotten. We can add all three to an existing pit.

Where the pump water is allowed to go

Pump water should go outside, away from the foundation, and not into the house drains. The township's sanitary sewers and septic systems are designed for household waste, and a sump pump can overload either one in a heavy rain. Ask the Randolph Division of Water and Sewer before connecting anything to a sanitary line.

If your home is on septic, stormwater in the tank can flood the drain field. If a basement floor drain has already backed up, sewer line cleaning looks at the main line, and a drain cleaning visit can open a clogged floor drain.

Water in the basement but the pump is fine

A pump that runs normally while the floor still gets wet means water is coming from somewhere else: a leaking pipe, a failing water heater, or a foundation crack. A bill that climbs while no one is home points the same way. Our leak detection service tracks these down with meters and sound equipment.
