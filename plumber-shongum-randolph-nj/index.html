Shongum is a named part of Randolph Township, Morris County, centered on Shongum Lake and Shongum Road. If you live here and have a clogged drain, a backed-up sewer line, a wet basement or a failing water heater, this page explains how we handle those calls and what to check first. Addresses here use Randolph, ZIP 07869.

We are a call-only service. You describe the problem to a dispatcher, and a licensed plumber is arranged for your address. There is nothing to fill out online.

The area

Where Shongum sits and how we describe it

The township's own landmark map places the Samuel Allen House, built around 1812, at 103 Shongum Road in the Shongum area. The township's parks plan lists Shongum Elementary School, a nine acre open space called The Glen at Shongum, and the private Shongum Lake Association. Those are the anchors we use, since we have not found a published boundary for Shongum.

That means we describe Shongum by its roads and landmarks and not by a drawn line. If you are not sure whether your street counts, tell the dispatcher the address. The answer for plumbing is the same, because we service all of Randolph.

Housing

Sewered single-family streets and what that means for a backup

An older land use section of the township master plan describes the Shongum Lake area, together with Mount Fern and Ironia, as sewered single-family neighborhoods in the R-2 and R-3 zones. Public sewer means a blockage can sit in your own line or in the connection to the main. Which part belongs to you is a township question.

Before you pay to dig, ask the Division of Water and Sewer where the township's responsibility ends at your address. A camera inspection shows where the blockage is, so the answer to that question is based on what the pipe actually looks like. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what that visit involves.

Lake setting

A lake neighborhood and a wet basement are a reasonable pairing to check

The township lists a private Shongum Lake Association, which tells us the lake has its own community. We do not have local data on groundwater or flooding in Shongum, so we make no claim about it. What we can say is that any house with a sump pit should test the pump before spring, by pouring water into the pit until the float lifts and the pump starts.

If the pump does not start, or it runs without moving water, that is a repair or a replacement. Do not wait for the next storm to find out.

Older homes

Remodels, permits and what is behind the wall

About 54 percent of Randolph's housing units were built before 1980, according to the township's housing plan, and the Samuel Allen House on Shongum Road dates to about 1812. Many Shongum homes have likely been remodeled at least once, though we have no count for the neighborhood.

A plumber can look and tell you what you have, so ask before you assume anything. Any change to piping or bathroom fixtures in Randolph needs a construction permit and zoning approval, so call the township construction office before work starts.
