Faucet repair in Randolph, NJ starts with a simple question: is the water still running? A dripping spout is a nuisance, but a valve that will not close or a supply line that has let go can be a flooding problem before you finish the phone call.

A faucet is a valve with a handle on it. Inside sit a cartridge, ball, disc or washer, plus rubber seals and a supply line. We send licensed plumbers who identify the style, replace the worn piece, and test the fixture under pressure before they leave.

Many faucets can be rebuilt. Some are cheaper to replace than to repair, especially when parts are no longer made.

Valves and supply lines

A shutoff valve that will not turn off

The small shutoff valves under sinks and behind toilets sit untouched for years. Mineral crust and corrosion freeze the stem, and forcing it can snap the handle or crack the packing. If a stop valve will not close, do not crank it with a wrench. Close the main valve for the house and call.

We replace failed stop valves and the braided or copper supply lines that connect to them. The job is short when the main can be closed and the pipe is sound, and longer when the pipe behind the valve is weak.

When a burst supply line is an emergency

A braided supply line that splits behind a vanity can empty a lot of water onto a floor in a short time, and the leak often starts when you are away or asleep. Spray from a connection, a bulging hose or a cabinet floor that suddenly feels wet all mean urgent.

Close the shutoff for that fixture if it works. If it does not, go to the main valve. After that, call the line.

What to do while we are on the way

1Shut it off at the closest valve

Try the fixture stop first, then the main. Turn clockwise by hand.

2Open one tap

Opening a tap on the lowest level drains the line and takes pressure off the break.

3Protect the room

Move rugs, lift stored boxes off the cabinet floor, and mop up to slow damage.

4Photograph the damage

Take photos before cleanup for your insurer.

Tub and shower faucet problems

A shower valve that drips after you turn it off, a tub spout that runs when the shower is on, or a wet wall behind the tile are all signs of a failing cartridge or diverter. Because shower valves sit inside the wall, a leak there can soak drywall for a long time before a stain shows.

We open the trim, pull the cartridge, and check the stem and seat. If water is reaching the wall cavity, we tell you before anyone closes it up again. Slow drains in the same tub are a separate job, and the same visit can cover toilet repair in that bathroom.

Price drivers

How faucet repair is priced

A drip and a hidden wall leak are not the same job, so a flat number would be dishonest. We give a clear price after we see the fixture and before we start.

Ball, cartridge, ceramic disc and compression faucets all rebuild differently. Parts for older or imported fixtures cost more to source.

If the body is corroded or the finish is failing, a new faucet costs less than repeated repairs.

Frozen stop valves add time and sometimes mean replacing the valve first.

Shower and tub valves need access through the wall or from the back side, which adds labor.

After-hours dispatch is priced differently from a daytime appointment.

A burst supply line often counts as sudden damage, while a drip that ran for months may be treated as maintenance. Policies vary, so read yours and ask what proof of date and cause the insurer expects.

Permit questions for fixture replacement

Randolph's permit page says that changing bathroom fixtures or piping needs a construction permit and zoning approval. State rules, though, exempt ordinary maintenance that includes routine repair, and the local enforcing agency has the last word. Replacing a washer is maintenance. Replacing a vanity with a new faucet and drain may not be.

The Office of Construction Codes takes calls during planning. If you are unsure, ask before the work starts, and do the same with the town office if you live in Denville or anywhere else, since each town runs its own.

A leak that is bigger than the faucet

A faucet that keeps dripping after a rebuild, a bill that climbs, or a damp cabinet with no visible source suggests the leak is in the pipe, not the fixture. Our leak detection service uses moisture meters and acoustic tools to find those. Under the kitchen sink, a leaking disposal can look just like a faucet problem, so we also repair a garbage disposal on the same visit.
